\title{Reduced Recurrent-State Persistence Reproduces Selected
Psilocybin-Related Working-Memory Signatures in Trained Recurrent Networks}

\author{Bob Rice}
\affiliation{University of Nottingham}

\begin{abstract}
Acute psilocybin does not produce a uniform loss of cognitive performance:
human studies instead report response slowing, greater impairment at higher
working-memory load, sensitivity to longer intervals, and vulnerability to
competing information. We asked whether any of seven abstract perturbations of
task-trained recurrent neural networks (RNNs) reproduced this selective
behavioural pattern. Five independently trained fixation-gated circular-memory
networks and ten independently trained N-back networks were evaluated on 1,024
trials or sequences per condition. We tested synaptic-drive gain,
heterogeneous drive gain, sensory-input gain, distractor-window gain,
recurrent gain, carried-state persistence, and a conserved effective
time-constant change. A carried-state persistence scale of 0.95 was the only
setting to reproduce the complete descriptive pattern. It increased
post-cue settling by $0.194\pm0.252$ model steps while changing ordinary
delay-20 angular error by only $1.38\pm6.17\%$; long-minus-short delay
selectivity was $0.264\pm0.140$, distractor-minus-clean selectivity was
$0.159\pm0.080$, and 2-back-minus-0-back selectivity was
$0.250\pm0.077$ (mean $\pm$ SD across trained seeds). Every contrast had the
predicted direction in all five circular or all ten N-back networks. However,
the slowing was small and the unperturbed distractor condition was weak.
Reduced state persistence is therefore computationally sufficient to produce
several selected cross-task signatures, but the result does not identify a
biological action of psilocybin or establish mechanistic uniqueness.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Working memory requires a neural system to preserve task-relevant information
after its eliciting input has disappeared. Recurrent excitation, attractor
dynamics, and slow manifolds provide candidate mechanisms through which an
internal state can remain stable while still supporting a later response
\cite{wang2013nmda,ghazizadeh2021}. A perturbation of these dynamics need not
destroy memory immediately. A weak change may instead expose selective
vulnerability when information must be maintained for longer, protected from
irrelevant input, or updated under greater task load.

The acute human behavioural literature motivates precisely such a selective
target. Psilocybin impaired 2-back performance more clearly than 0-back
performance in a letter N-back task \cite{barrett2018}. It also impaired
multiple-object tracking while spatial working-memory span was comparatively
spared in a small within-subject study \cite{carter2005}. Longer interval
reproduction was more affected than shorter intervals, with spatial span
impairment emerging at the higher dose \cite{wittmann2007}. In a
memory-guided delayed-response task, reaction time increased without a clear
reduction in correct responses \cite{vollenweider1998}. A recent meta-analysis
similarly found a more robust acute effect on reaction time than accuracy
across attention and executive tasks, while cautioning that the evidence was
heterogeneous and at risk of bias \cite{yousefi2025}. Together, these findings
define qualitative dissociations rather than a single target level of
performance.

Candidate network manipulations were motivated by, but not equated with,
psychedelic mechanisms. REBUS relates relaxed high-level precision to altered
postsynaptic gain and afferent sensitivity \cite{carhartharris2019}; a
receptor-informed whole-brain model operationalised 5-HT2A effects through
spatially heterogeneous response gain \cite{herzog2023}. Neither framework
specifies a unique perturbation of a trained working-memory RNN. We therefore
treated gain, recurrent weighting, sensory sensitivity, state persistence, and
effective timescale as competing computational interventions.

We evaluated these interventions in two complementary task families. The
fixation-gated circular task measured response settling, delay dependence, and
distractor sensitivity. The N-back task measured selective impairment at
higher updating load. We hypothesised that a credible candidate would reproduce
several dissociations across independently trained networks rather than merely
increase average error. This study is a candidate-versus-native-baseline
analysis; generic-noise matching and biological identification were outside
its scope.

\section{Methods}

\subsection{Fixation-gated circular delayed-response task}

Each circular-task trial comprised pre-cue fixation, a circular cue, a silent
memory delay, and a response period (Fig.~\ref{fig:tasks}A). Stimulus angle
$\theta\in[0,2\pi)$ was encoded over 32 evenly spaced preferred angles
$\mu_j$ as
\begin{equation}
 c_j(\theta)=\exp\{\kappa[\cos(\theta-\mu_j)-1]\},\qquad \kappa=8.
\end{equation}
The fixation input and output remained high until the response cue. Circular
outputs were deliberately silent before response and then reported the
remembered angle. Pre-cue and cue durations varied during training; evaluation
used clean delays of 10, 20, 40, and 80 model steps. A separate delay-20
condition inserted a five-step irrelevant circular cue midway through the
delay. The networks had not been trained with this distractor, making it an
out-of-distribution robustness probe rather than a second trained task.

\subsection{Context-cued N-back task}

The N-back task presented 20 sequential items drawn from six stimulus
identities (Fig.~\ref{fig:tasks}B). Two context channels instructed either
0-back or 2-back behaviour. In 0-back, the network classified whether the
current item matched a fixed target identity. In 2-back, it classified whether
the current item matched the item presented two positions previously.
Sequences contained six matches; the 2-back generator additionally controlled
one-back lures. The first two items were excluded from scored outcomes. The
output consisted of match and non-match logits.

\subsection{Recurrent networks and checkpoint replication}

Both task families used 64-unit \texttt{tanh} recurrent networks with
$dt=20$, $\tau=100$, and $\alpha=dt/\tau=0.2$. The native update was
\begin{align}
 z_t &= W_{\mathrm{in}}x_t+b_{\mathrm{in}}
      +W_{\mathrm{rec}}h_{t-1}+b_{\mathrm{rec}},\\
 h_t &= \tanh\!\left[(1-\alpha)h_{t-1}+\alpha z_t\right].
 \label{eq:native}
\end{align}
The circular model had 33 inputs and outputs: 32 tuned channels plus
fixation. The N-back model had six stimulus inputs, two rule-context inputs,
and two outputs. Perturbations were applied only at evaluation; no model was
retrained. Five competent circular checkpoints (seeds
20260714--20260718) and ten competent N-back checkpoints (seeds
20260912--20260921) were retained. Independently trained checkpoint, not trial
or technical replicate, was the replication unit.

\subsection{Candidate perturbations}

We evaluated seven single-operator families (Table~\ref{tab:perturbations}).
For synaptic-drive gain, the weight-derived input and recurrent drive was
multiplied by scalar $g$, while learned biases remained outside the gain. Fixed
heterogeneous gain replaced $g$ with a positive unit-wise vector
$\mathbf{g}$ drawn from a log-normal distribution and normalised to population
mean one; three fixed vectors were treated as technical replicates and averaged
within checkpoint. Sensory-input gain scaled only stimulus-channel input
drive, leaving rule and fixation channels unchanged. Distractor-window gain
applied this operation only during the irrelevant circular cue. Recurrent gain
scaled $W_{\mathrm{rec}}h_{t-1}$ while leaving recurrent bias unchanged.

State persistence changed the carried-state term but retained the native drive
coefficient:
\begin{equation}
 h_t=\tanh\!\left[p(1-\alpha)h_{t-1}+\alpha z_t\right],
 \label{eq:persistence}
\end{equation}
where $p$ is the persistence scale. This is an asymmetric intervention and is
not identical to response gain. The conserved time-constant operator instead
set $\alpha'=\min(\alpha/s,1)$ and used
\begin{equation}
 h_t=\tanh\!\left[(1-\alpha')h_{t-1}+\alpha'z_t\right],
\end{equation}
so carried-state and drive coefficients continued to sum to one.

\subsection{Evaluation and behavioural signature}

Each circular checkpoint, operator, strength, condition, and delay used 1,024
trials. Each N-back checkpoint, operator, strength, and load used 1,024
20-item sequences. Native and perturbed evaluations reused identical generated
task samples within checkpoint and cell.

Circular response accuracy was mean absolute angular error. Post-cue settling
was the first response step at which angular error was below $15^\circ$ and
population-vector amplitude exceeded 50\% of the native reference amplitude.
Trials that did not settle were capped at the 25-step response duration,
yielding restricted mean settling time. Settling was interpreted only when
fixation accuracy was at least 0.90 and at least 80\% of trials settled.
``Slowing with preservation'' required a positive settling change at clean
delay 20 while proportional angular-error impairment remained at or below
0.20.

For circular condition $c$, proportional impairment was
\begin{equation}
 I_c=\frac{E_{\mathrm{perturbed},c}-E_{\mathrm{native},c}}
 {E_{\mathrm{native},c}},
\end{equation}
where $E$ is mean angular error. Delay selectivity was
$I_{\mathrm{delay\,80}}-I_{\mathrm{delay\,10}}$; distractor selectivity was
$I_{\mathrm{distractor\,20}}-I_{\mathrm{clean\,20}}$.

N-back discriminability was $D=H-F$, where $H$ and $F$ are hit and false-alarm
rates. Condition-normalised impairment was
\begin{equation}
 J_c=\frac{D_{\mathrm{native},c}-D_{\mathrm{perturbed},c}}
 {D_{\mathrm{native},c}},
\end{equation}
and load selectivity was $J_{\mathrm{2back}}-J_{\mathrm{0back}}$. Positive
values indicate disproportionate high-load impairment.

The analysis was descriptive. For each operator-strength setting we reported
the mean contrast and fraction of checkpoint seeds in the predicted direction.
A complete pattern required positive mean slowing with preservation,
distractor selectivity, and N-back load selectivity, with a majority of
checkpoint seeds agreeing; delay selectivity supplied additional supporting
evidence. No participant-level inference, confirmatory $p$ values, or
multiple-comparison claims were made.

\section{Results}

\subsection{The full screen isolated one complete cross-task profile}

The perturbations were not interchangeable. Across 29 shared
operator-strength profiles, only state persistence $p=0.95$ reproduced the
complete descriptive pattern (Fig.~\ref{fig:screen}). It showed slowing with
preservation, long-delay selectivity, and distractor selectivity in all five
circular checkpoints, together with load selectivity in all ten N-back
checkpoints. Neutral settings returned zero contrast. Other perturbations often
reproduced one or two components, but not the full combination.

\subsection{A 5\% persistence reduction preserved ordinary accuracy while
selectively affecting demanding conditions}

At $p=0.95$, clean delay-20 angular error changed by
$1.38\pm6.17\%$ across circular checkpoints, satisfying the preservation
criterion in all five seeds (Table~\ref{tab:leading}). Restricted mean settling
increased by $0.194\pm0.252$ steps. Every seed showed a positive change, but
the range was only 0.010--0.561 steps; the result is therefore a small,
replicated response-dynamics analogue rather than a large reaction-time effect.

Long-minus-short delay selectivity was $0.264\pm0.140$ and positive in 5/5
checkpoints. Distractor-minus-clean selectivity was $0.159\pm0.080$ and also
positive in 5/5. In the N-back networks, 2-back-minus-0-back selectivity was
$0.250\pm0.077$, positive in 10/10 checkpoints, and ranged from 0.110 to
0.363. Thus the leading perturbation did not merely increase average error: it
preferentially affected longer maintenance, competing input, and higher
updating load.

\subsection{The persistence effect was dose-like but not monotonic across all
features}

Reducing persistence further to 0.90 enlarged mean settling, delay, and N-back
load effects (Fig.~\ref{fig:persistence}). However, slowing with preservation
passed only 2/5 circular checkpoints, so the stronger intervention no longer
expressed the selective accuracy-preserving pattern. Increasing persistence
above one continued to impair N-back preferentially but reversed the circular
delay and distractor contrasts. The joint signature was therefore specific to
a weak reduction around 0.95 rather than monotonic damage at any non-neutral
strength.

\subsection{Alternative operators reproduced partial signatures}

Sensory-input gain 1.20 produced distractor selectivity in 5/5 circular
checkpoints and load selectivity in 9/10 N-back checkpoints, but mean settling
became slightly faster and delay selectivity was inconsistent. Effective
time-constant scale 0.90 produced delay and distractor selectivity but also
accelerated settling. Recurrent gain 0.95 showed delay selectivity in 4/5 and
load selectivity in 9/10 checkpoints, but distractor selectivity in only 1/5.
Heterogeneous drive gain frequently produced delay and N-back effects without
distractor selectivity. These near-misses constrain the result: matching one
difficult condition was insufficient to qualify as the complete pattern.

\subsection{The distractor contrast remained validity-limited}

All 945 circular result rows passed the fixation gate, all ten N-back native
checkpoints passed competence, and all $p=0.95$ clean delay-20 cells were
latency-valid. However, the unperturbed distractor condition was already weak:
mean angular error ranged from $25.8^\circ$ to $50.4^\circ$, and only
26.7--35.7\% of native distractor trials settled. Increasing the trial count
reduced sampling uncertainty but could not repair this out-of-distribution
baseline. The distractor direction is reproducible, but its magnitude may
reflect floor or headroom effects and cannot support a strong Carter-style
recovery claim in isolation.

\section{Discussion}

The full candidate screen identified reduced carried-state persistence as the
strongest computational account of the selected cross-task behavioural
pattern. Scaling the carried-state coefficient by 0.95 left ordinary circular
accuracy approximately intact while modestly slowing response settling,
increasing vulnerability at longer delays and under distraction, and
selectively impairing 2-back discriminability. The result replicated across
all five circular and all ten N-back checkpoints.

Equation~\ref{eq:persistence} suggests a coherent model-level explanation.
Each update retains slightly less of the preceding hidden state without
simultaneously rescaling incoming drive. Information is therefore not erased
at once; rather, its stability is weakened cumulatively. Short and simple
conditions remain relatively intact, whereas longer maintenance, competing
input, and repeated updating expose the loss. This interpretation is supported
by the delay and load contrasts, although the present behavioural analyses do
not yet directly demonstrate a changed attractor or slow manifold.

Importantly, ``state persistence'' is an operational property of this
implemented recurrence, not a measured neurobiological parameter. The result
shows computational sufficiency: this intervention can generate the selected
qualitative pattern. It does not show that psilocybin reduces neural
persistence, that 5-HT2A activation maps to $p=0.95$, or that the mechanism is
unique. A generic-damage comparator was deliberately postponed, and several
alternative operators reproduced individual components. Subsequent analyses
should therefore examine hidden-state drift and stability and, separately,
test specificity against non-neutral matched-cost controls.

Two limitations particularly constrain the present inference. First, post-cue
settling is not literal reaction time because response onset was externally
imposed; moreover, the mean change was less than one model step. Second, the
distractor condition lacked a competent native baseline. A weaker irrelevant
cue or a separately trained distractor-capable task is required before the
Carter-style dissociation can be treated as secure. The descriptive grid also
contains multiple operator-strength profiles but no confirmatory inferential
family. Checkpoint consistency demonstrates robustness across trained models,
not population inference about human participants.

The immediate next experiment should densify persistence scaling around
0.95, quantify delay-period representational drift, and repeat the clean,
delay, and N-back contrasts on independently banked task samples. The
distractor amplitude should first be calibrated to retain usable native
performance. A later specificity analysis can compare the retained candidate
with generic perturbation at comparable clean-task cost without changing the
candidate-versus-baseline result reported here.

\section*{Conclusion}

A 5\% reduction in carried-state persistence was the only tested RNN
perturbation to reproduce the complete pre-specified descriptive pattern:
relative preservation with slower settling, greater long-delay and distractor
costs, and selective 2-back impairment. The consistency across independently
trained networks makes reduced persistence a credible candidate for further
mechanistic analysis. The result nevertheless remains an abstract
computational sufficiency finding, with small settling effects and a
validity-limited distractor condition, rather than evidence for a biological
mechanism of psilocybin.
